Breath Play

Breath play is an umbrella term for activities that involve restricting, controlling, or interfering with someone's breathing or oxygen supply during sexual or BDSM activity. It is sometimes called erotic asphyxiation, choking, or breath control.

Important: Breath Play Can Cause Serious Injury or Death

There is no reliably safe way to restrict another person's breathing or blood flow to the brain. Pressure on the neck, airway obstruction, and oxygen deprivation can cause stroke, permanent brain injury, loss of consciousness, cardiac complications, or death.

Serious harm can happen unexpectedly, even when an activity is brief, seemingly gentle, or performed by people who trust each other. There may be no visible injury, and dangerous complications can appear after the activity has ended.

Consent, experience, safewords, communication, and aftercare do not eliminate these medical risks. This page is educational, not a guide to performing breath play.

Understanding Breath Play

Breath play describes a range of activities in which breathing, airflow, or oxygen delivery becomes part of a sexual or power-exchange experience. The terminology can vary between communities and individuals.

Activities sometimes described as breath play include:

  • Neck compression: Applying pressure to the neck. This is commonly called choking, but medically it is a form of strangulation.
  • Airway obstruction: Activities that interfere with the ability to draw air into the lungs.
  • Smothering: Covering or obstructing the nose or mouth.
  • Breath restriction: Deliberately interfering with normal breathing.
  • Self-directed asphyxiation: Activities in which someone attempts to restrict their own breathing or oxygen supply.

These activities do not all create identical medical risks, but any intentional restriction of breathing or oxygen delivery can be dangerous. Neck compression is especially concerning because it can damage blood vessels and other structures even without completely blocking the airway.

Why Are People Interested in Breath Play?

People may be drawn to breath play for different psychological, emotional, or physical reasons. Understanding the appeal does not make the activity medically safe.

  • Power exchange: Some people associate breath control with dominance, submission, vulnerability, or surrender.
  • Trust and intimacy: The perceived vulnerability may be connected to feelings of closeness or trust.
  • Psychological intensity: Some are drawn to the intensity, anticipation, or emotional experience.
  • Fantasy and roleplay: The appearance or symbolism of breath control may be part of a negotiated scene.
  • Media influence: Pornography, social media, and popular culture sometimes portray choking as routine or harmless, creating unrealistic expectations.

It is important to separate the fantasy from the physical act. Someone may enjoy the symbolism, language, or psychological elements of a scene without accepting the medical risks of actual breathing restriction.

Why Breath Play Is So Dangerous

The brain depends on a continuous supply of oxygen-rich blood. Interfering with breathing or blood flow can quickly become a medical emergency. The body does not provide a dependable warning system that tells someone exactly when an activity is becoming dangerous.

Neck compression and strangulation

Pressure on the neck can affect several critical structures, including blood vessels supplying the brain, the airway, nerves, and surrounding tissues. Damage can occur without obvious external marks.

Oxygen deprivation

When the brain does not receive enough oxygen, a person can become confused, lose coordination, lose consciousness, suffer seizures, or sustain permanent neurological injury. Severe oxygen deprivation can be fatal.

Blood vessel damage and stroke

Compression or injury to blood vessels in the neck can cause damage to arterial walls or the formation of blood clots. These complications can lead to a stroke, including one that occurs after the original incident.

Airway injury and swelling

Injury to the throat or airway can cause swelling, difficulty breathing, voice changes, or delayed airway obstruction. Someone may initially appear to be recovering while an injury is developing.

Cardiac complications

Oxygen deprivation and trauma can contribute to abnormal heart rhythms, cardiac arrest, and other life-threatening complications.

Permanent injury or death

The consequences can include permanent brain damage, cognitive impairment, memory problems, neurological disability, stroke, and death. A person does not have to lose consciousness for serious injury to occur.

There is no dependable duration, pressure level, or technique that makes strangulation or oxygen deprivation safe.

Common Misconceptions

One of the biggest problems with breath play is that popular portrayals often make it appear predictable and controllable. These assumptions can lead people to underestimate the danger.

"If it is gentle, it is safe."

There is no reliably safe level of neck compression. Even pressure that appears minor can cause internal injury.

"If the person can still talk, they are okay."

Being able to speak does not establish that blood flow is normal or that there is no injury. A person can experience serious damage without immediately losing the ability to communicate.

"If there are no marks, there is no injury."

Internal injuries may occur without bruising, redness, or visible marks. The absence of external evidence is not reassurance.

"A safeword makes it safe."

A person experiencing oxygen deprivation or neurological impairment may become confused, lose the ability to communicate, or lose consciousness. They may be unable to use a safeword or signal.

"An experienced Dominant can control the risk."

Experience does not eliminate the unpredictable effects of neck compression or oxygen deprivation. Knowledge of BDSM, anatomy, or negotiation cannot guarantee a safe outcome.

"If someone seems fine afterward, there is no problem."

Some complications can be delayed. A person may develop symptoms hours or days after an incident, even if they initially felt normal.

Consent, Negotiation, and Boundaries

Consent is essential in BDSM, but it should never be confused with a guarantee of physical safety. A person can willingly accept a risk without being able to predict or control the resulting injury.

Before any scene involving breath-related themes, partners should have an honest conversation about boundaries, expectations, and alternatives. This conversation should happen well before sexual activity begins.

Topics to discuss

  • What does each person mean when they use the term breath play?
  • Are they interested in the psychological fantasy, the appearance, or an actual physical activity?
  • What activities are completely off limits?
  • Are there alternative ways to explore the same power dynamic without restricting breathing or blood flow?
  • Does either person feel pressured to participate to satisfy a partner?
  • How will either person communicate discomfort or withdraw consent?
  • What is the plan if someone becomes distressed or experiences a medical emergency?

Consent must remain voluntary

No one should feel obligated to participate because a partner requests it, because it is common in pornography, or because they have previously agreed to something similar.

Consent can be withdrawn at any time. A previous agreement does not create an obligation to continue.

If someone is uncomfortable with breath play, that boundary should be respected without negotiation intended to wear them down.

Safer Alternatives to Explore the Fantasy

Some of the psychological elements people associate with breath play can be explored without interfering with breathing or blood flow. These alternatives do not guarantee that every scene is risk-free, but they avoid the specific dangers of strangulation and oxygen deprivation.

  • Verbal power exchange: Explore commands, permission, control, and submission through language.
  • Roleplay: Use agreed-upon scenarios to explore vulnerability, intensity, or surrender without physical restriction.
  • Symbolic gestures: Use agreed-upon gestures or non-restrictive touch to represent a fantasy without applying pressure to the neck or airway.
  • Restraint alternatives: Explore consensual restraint that does not involve the neck, chest, or restriction of breathing.
  • Sensory play: Explore anticipation, attention, and heightened awareness through other forms of sensation.
  • Negotiated scene dynamics: Create a power-exchange experience centered on trust, control, and communication rather than physiological deprivation.

The goal is not to reproduce the physical effects of breath restriction. It is to identify what someone enjoys about the fantasy and explore those elements without deliberately compromising a vital bodily function.

Recognizing a Medical Emergency

Following neck compression or an incident involving restricted breathing, serious symptoms may appear immediately or develop later. Do not assume that someone is safe because they are conscious or able to communicate.

Warning signs include:

  • Difficulty breathing, shortness of breath, or noisy breathing
  • Changes in voice, hoarseness, or difficulty speaking
  • Difficulty or pain when swallowing
  • Neck pain, tenderness, or swelling
  • Dizziness, confusion, disorientation, or memory problems
  • Severe or unusual headache
  • Blurred vision, visual disturbances, or hearing changes
  • Weakness, numbness, or loss of coordination
  • Seizures or loss of consciousness
  • Unusual bruising, small red spots, or bloodshot eyes

When in Doubt, Seek Emergency Medical Care

If someone loses consciousness, has difficulty breathing, experiences seizure activity, develops neurological symptoms, or shows signs of a possible stroke, call 911 immediately.

After neck compression, prompt medical evaluation is important even if there are no visible marks or obvious symptoms. Do not wait for symptoms to appear or worsen.

What to Do After a Breath-Related Incident

If an incident occurs, treat it as a potential medical emergency rather than an ordinary BDSM scene that simply needs aftercare.

  1. Stop the activity immediately. Do not resume the scene or attempt to recreate what happened.
  2. Call 911 when there are emergency symptoms. Be clear that the person experienced neck compression, breathing restriction, or possible oxygen deprivation.
  3. Do not leave the person alone. Monitor their responsiveness and breathing while waiting for emergency responders.
  4. Follow emergency dispatcher instructions. If the person is unresponsive and not breathing normally, begin CPR and use an AED if available, following dispatcher guidance and your training.
  5. Seek medical evaluation. Neck compression can cause hidden injuries, even when the person appears to have recovered.
  6. Be honest with medical professionals. Explain what happened, including any neck pressure, breathing restriction, loss of consciousness, or symptoms. Accurate information helps clinicians assess potential injuries.

Do not substitute water, food, rest, reassurance, or ordinary aftercare for medical evaluation. Do not assume that someone who wakes up or begins breathing normally has avoided injury.

Aftercare and Recovery

Emotional aftercare is an important part of BDSM, but it cannot treat internal injuries caused by strangulation or oxygen deprivation.

If a breath-related incident has occurred, medical evaluation takes priority over cuddling, reassurance, hydration, or other traditional aftercare practices.

People involved may also experience fear, guilt, anxiety, embarrassment, or other emotional reactions. These feelings deserve attention, but they should never prevent someone from seeking medical help.

If symptoms develop or change after an incident, seek urgent medical attention. Do not dismiss new headaches, voice changes, swallowing difficulties, confusion, or breathing problems.

Getting Started: An Important Distinction

For people new to BDSM, it is important to understand that breath play is not simply another beginner activity to learn through practice. It involves potentially life-threatening physiological risks.

A class, workshop, online tutorial, experienced partner, or safety checklist cannot make strangulation or intentional oxygen deprivation reliably safe.

If the attraction is the psychological dynamic, consider exploring verbal control, roleplay, sensory play, and other alternatives that do not interfere with breathing or blood flow.

If a partner requests breath play and you are uncomfortable, you do not need to justify your decision. Saying no is enough.

Final Thoughts

Breath play is one of the highest-risk activities discussed in BDSM communities. It can cause severe injury or death, sometimes without obvious warning signs or visible injuries.

Trust, consent, experience, and communication are essential to BDSM, but they cannot override human physiology. No amount of negotiation or experience can guarantee that restricting someone's breathing or blood flow will be safe.

Understanding the risks, recognizing warning signs, and knowing when to seek emergency medical care are essential. There are many ways to explore dominance, submission, vulnerability, and intensity without deliberately putting someone's ability to breathe at risk.

Consent matters. Safety matters. And breathing is not something to gamble with.
