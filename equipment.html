Introduction to Dungeon Equipment

BDSM dungeons often contain a wide variety of specialized furniture, restraint systems, suspension equipment, and other devices designed to support different forms of consensual play. For someone visiting a dungeon for the first time, the equipment can be both fascinating and intimidating.

Some pieces of equipment are designed to position the body for impact play, while others provide restraint, support suspension, or create opportunities for different types of sensation and power exchange. Many pieces serve multiple purposes depending on their design and intended use.

Understanding what equipment is designed to do is important. A piece of furniture that appears sturdy is not necessarily designed to support someone's full body weight, and a restraint system that looks simple may introduce significant circulation, nerve, or positioning risks.

This guide introduces common types of dungeon equipment, their intended applications, general safety considerations, and the importance of proper cleaning and maintenance.

Important: Not all dungeon equipment is suitable for every person or activity. Always follow manufacturer instructions, venue rules, equipment ratings, and appropriate training requirements. Never assume that appearance alone indicates structural safety.

Common Dungeon Furniture and Equipment

Dungeon furniture is often designed around particular body positions, restraint configurations, and styles of play. Some equipment is relatively straightforward, while other pieces require specialized knowledge and experience.

1. St. Andrew's Cross

A St. Andrew's Cross is one of the most recognizable pieces of BDSM dungeon furniture. It typically consists of two padded or unpadded beams arranged in an X shape, supported by a stable frame.

The cross is designed to allow someone to stand facing the structure with their arms and legs positioned along the diagonal beams. Depending on the design, attachment points may be provided for wrist and ankle restraints.

St. Andrew's crosses are commonly used for:

  • Impact play: Positioning someone for consensual spanking, flogging, paddling, and other forms of impact.
  • Restraint: Providing attachment locations for compatible wrist and ankle restraints.
  • Sensation play: Allowing a partner to explore different forms of touch, sensation, and stimulation.
  • Power exchange: Creating a position that can be incorporated into consensual dominance and submission dynamics.

Crosses may be constructed from wood, metal, or combinations of materials. Some have padded surfaces, while others use exposed structural beams.

Proper use: Check that the structure is stable, inspect its attachment points, and ensure that restraints are compatible with the equipment. Participants should be able to communicate throughout the scene, and restraint configurations must not compromise circulation, breathing, or safe positioning.

Pay particular attention to shoulder positioning. Holding the arms extended for long periods can create discomfort, nerve compression, and joint strain. Avoid forcing someone into a position that exceeds their comfortable range of motion.

2. Spanking Benches

A spanking bench is designed to support someone in a forward-leaning or kneeling position. It typically has a padded upper surface, a support structure, and sometimes attachment points for restraints.

The design allows the recipient to maintain a relatively consistent position while their partner performs impact or sensation play.

Common variations include:

  • Traditional spanking benches: Provide a padded surface for the upper body while the recipient kneels or stands.
  • Adjustable benches: Allow the height or angle of the support surface to be changed.
  • Restraint benches: Include attachment points for wrist, ankle, or other compatible restraints.
  • Combination furniture: Incorporate bench-style positioning with other restraint or impact-play features.

Spanking benches are frequently used for spanking, paddling, flogging, and other forms of consensual impact play.

Proper use: Ensure that the recipient can maintain a comfortable position without excessive pressure on their knees, hips, shoulders, or lower back. Check the padding and restraints before use.

Avoid positioning that restricts breathing or places excessive pressure on the abdomen or chest. The recipient should be able to communicate discomfort and request an immediate change in position.

3. Hard Points and Attachment Points

Hard points are structural attachment locations designed to connect compatible restraint or suspension equipment to a supporting structure.

They may be installed in ceilings, walls, floors, structural frames, or specialized dungeon furniture.

Hard points can be used for:

  • Attaching compatible restraint systems.
  • Connecting approved suspension hardware.
  • Supporting designated dungeon equipment.
  • Providing attachment locations for specialized play furniture.

Hard points are not interchangeable. An attachment intended for a light restraint application may not be appropriate for supporting a person's body weight.

Proper use: Never assume a hard point is load-bearing because it is attached to a ceiling or wall. Its intended purpose, structural installation, hardware, condition, and rated working load must be established.

Suspension requires appropriately engineered and inspected attachment systems. Ordinary eye bolts, hooks, decorative hardware, and improvised mounting solutions should never be assumed suitable for supporting a suspended person.

Hard points should be inspected periodically for looseness, corrosion, deformation, cracking, and other signs of damage. Installation and structural suitability should be assessed by qualified professionals.

4. Stocks

Stocks are restraint furniture historically associated with confinement and punishment. Traditional designs use a wooden or metal structure with openings that secure the head, wrists, ankles, or other body areas.

Modern BDSM stocks vary considerably. Some are compact tabletop devices, while others are larger freestanding structures.

Stocks may be incorporated into:

  • Consensual immobilization.
  • Power exchange and roleplay.
  • Impact and sensation scenes.
  • Psychological BDSM experiences.

Proper use: Stocks can create significant risks because they may restrict movement and make it difficult for someone to reposition themselves independently.

Ensure that the equipment fits the participant without excessive pressure, particularly around the neck, wrists, and other areas where nerves and blood vessels may be compressed.

The head and neck should never be placed under unsafe mechanical restraint. Participants must have an effective communication method and a reliable way for the scene to be stopped and the restraints released promptly.

5. Spreader Bars

Spreader bars are restraint accessories designed to maintain separation between two limbs. They commonly have attachment points at either end for compatible wrist or ankle restraints.

Some are rigid metal or wooden bars, while others use adjustable designs that allow different spacing.

Spreader bars may be used for:

  • Positioning the arms or legs.
  • Incorporating restraint into power exchange scenes.
  • Limiting certain types of movement.
  • Combining with compatible furniture or restraint systems.

Proper use: A spreader bar can place substantial stress on joints, particularly when someone is positioned with their legs or arms farther apart than their natural range of motion.

Avoid forcing limbs into extreme positions. Consider flexibility, previous injuries, joint conditions, and individual comfort.

Participants should not be left in a position where they cannot communicate discomfort or where a restraint cannot be released promptly.

6. Cages

BDSM cages are enclosures designed to create a confined or restricted environment as part of consensual roleplay and power exchange.

Cages may be constructed from metal, wood, or other materials. They range from relatively small decorative enclosures to larger structures designed to accommodate a person in a seated or kneeling position.

Common applications include:

  • Consensual confinement and immobilization.
  • Pet play and roleplay.
  • Dominance and submission dynamics.
  • Psychological restraint and sensory experiences.

Proper use: A cage should never be treated as a substitute for a supervised and negotiated restraint system.

Ensure that the occupant has adequate ventilation, room to maintain a safe posture, and a means of communicating. The enclosure must not create entrapment hazards or prevent emergency access.

Avoid prolonged confinement, particularly in positions that place pressure on joints or restrict circulation. Never lock someone inside a cage without a readily available means of emergency release.

7. Suspension Frames and Rigs

Suspension frames are specialized structures designed to support suspension equipment. They may be freestanding or installed as part of a permanent dungeon structure.

Suspension frames are commonly used in rope bondage and other forms of suspension play where participants are supported above the ground.

Common configurations include:

  • A-frame rigs: Freestanding frames with two supporting sides and an overhead attachment structure.
  • Goalpost rigs: Structures with upright supports and a horizontal overhead beam.
  • Tripod rigs: Three-legged structures designed for specific suspension applications.
  • Permanent suspension structures: Professionally installed structures integrated into a dedicated dungeon space.

Suspension can involve significant mechanical forces. The combined weight of the participant, movement, loading direction, and equipment configuration all affect structural requirements.

Proper use: Suspension is an advanced activity requiring appropriate training, specialized equipment, and a thorough understanding of the risks involved.

Frames, connection points, ropes, hardware, and other components must be appropriate for the intended application. A frame that is suitable for one configuration should not automatically be assumed suitable for another.

Never improvise suspension structures or rely on decorative hardware, furniture, or unverified attachment points. Suspension should not be attempted without the necessary skills and safety preparation.

8. BDSM Swings

BDSM swings are suspended seating or support systems designed to position the body in different orientations. They may be used for sensation play, intimacy, positioning, and consensual power exchange.

Some swings have a hammock-like design, while others use multiple straps, supports, and attachment points.

Common designs include:

  • Hammock swings: Provide a fabric or padded surface that supports the body.
  • Strap swings: Use multiple straps to support different parts of the body.
  • Positioning swings: Include specialized supports intended to accommodate particular body positions.
  • Combination swings: Incorporate restraint attachment points or adjustable support components.

Proper use: Swings introduce risks associated with suspended body weight, movement, pressure points, and the possibility of falling.

Verify the swing's rated capacity, mounting system, attachment hardware, and condition. Ensure that the participant can be safely supported and released.

Pay particular attention to pressure on the neck, chest, abdomen, joints, and areas where nerves and blood vessels may be compressed.

9. Bondage Beds and Restraint Tables

Bondage beds and restraint tables are specialized pieces of furniture designed to support participants in lying or partially reclined positions.

They may include padded surfaces, restraint attachment locations, adjustable sections, and other specialized features.

These structures are commonly used for:

  • Full-body restraint.
  • Sensation and impact play.
  • Medical roleplay.
  • Massage and other forms of consensual touch.
  • Positioning for specialized scenes.

Proper use: Check the structural integrity of the bed or table, its weight rating, and the condition of any restraint attachment points.

Be especially careful with positioning that places pressure on the chest, abdomen, or neck. Participants must be able to communicate and have an appropriate means of being released.

10. Pillories and Vertical Restraint Furniture

Pillories are restraint structures traditionally designed to secure a person's head and sometimes their hands in a fixed position.

Modern BDSM versions may be standalone furniture, tabletop structures, or components incorporated into larger restraint systems.

They may be used in consensual roleplay, psychological BDSM, and immobilization scenes.

Proper use: Restraint around the head and neck requires particular caution. Avoid any configuration that creates pressure on the airway, restricts blood flow, or places the cervical spine in an unsafe position.

A participant must never be left unable to communicate discomfort or request immediate release.

11. Restraint Chairs

Restraint chairs are specialized chairs designed with attachment locations for securing the arms, legs, or torso.

They may be used for consensual immobilization, roleplay, sensation play, and other activities where a seated position is desired.

Some designs include adjustable restraints, padded surfaces, and reclining features.

Proper use: Ensure that restraints do not create excessive pressure on wrists, ankles, shoulders, or other joints. Avoid positions that restrict breathing or circulation.

Participants should be monitored and should have a reliable communication and release method throughout the scene.

12. Other Dungeon Equipment

Always establish what a piece of equipment is designed to do before using it. A piece of furniture that looks appropriate for an activity may not have the necessary structural or safety characteristics.

Understanding Load Ratings and Structural Safety

One of the most important aspects of dungeon equipment safety is understanding the difference between furniture designed to support someone and equipment designed to support someone under dynamic or suspended loading.

A piece of furniture may comfortably support someone sitting or leaning against it but still be unsuitable for suspension or significant pulling forces.

Understanding Load Ratings

  • Working Load Limit (WLL): The maximum load that a component is rated to support under its specified conditions of use.
  • Breaking strength: The load at which a component is expected to fail under specified testing conditions. This is not an acceptable operating load.
  • Static loading: A load that remains relatively constant without significant movement.
  • Dynamic loading: Forces created by movement, shifting weight, acceleration, or sudden changes in loading.
  • Load direction: Equipment may have different ratings depending on whether forces are applied vertically, horizontally, or at an angle.

Dynamic loading can create forces significantly different from those associated with simply supporting a person's stationary weight.

A component's rating does not automatically establish the safety of the entire system. Every part of the configuration, including mounting hardware, connections, structural supports, and the equipment itself, must be appropriate for the intended application.

Never exceed manufacturer ratings, combine incompatible components, or assume that a strong-looking structure is safe for suspension.

Inspecting Dungeon Equipment Before Use

Equipment should be inspected before every use. Regular maintenance is important, but even equipment that was recently inspected can become damaged or compromised.

What to Look For

  • Structural damage: Cracks, splits, bent components, loose joints, or visible deformation.
  • Loose hardware: Missing fasteners, loose bolts, damaged connectors, or compromised mounting points.
  • Metal corrosion: Rust, pitting, or deterioration that may compromise the strength of metal components.
  • Wood deterioration: Splintering, cracking, water damage, or weakened joints.
  • Padding damage: Torn upholstery, exposed foam, damaged stitching, or compromised protective surfaces.
  • Restraint damage: Frayed straps, damaged stitching, worn leather, compromised buckles, or damaged adjustment mechanisms.
  • Moving components: Check that hinges, locking mechanisms, and adjustable sections function as intended.

If equipment appears damaged, unstable, or questionable, do not use it. Report the issue to the dungeon operator and make sure it is taken out of service until it has been properly assessed and repaired.

Do not attempt temporary repairs to load-bearing equipment with tape, improvised fasteners, or other makeshift solutions.

Cleaning and Maintaining Dungeon Equipment

Dungeons are shared environments, and equipment may be used by many different people throughout an event. Proper cleaning is important for hygiene, infection prevention, and maintaining the condition of the equipment.

Different materials require different cleaning methods. A cleaning product that is appropriate for metal may damage leather, wood, upholstery, or specialty materials.

1. Cleaning Between Participants

Shared equipment should be cleaned between users according to the venue's procedures and the manufacturer's instructions.

  • Remove visible dirt, sweat, and bodily fluids using an appropriate cleaning method.
  • Use a compatible disinfectant when required.
  • Follow the product's required contact time rather than wiping disinfectant away immediately.
  • Allow surfaces to dry completely when required.
  • Replace disposable protective coverings between users.
  • Dispose of used wipes, gloves, and other cleaning materials appropriately.

Cleaning and disinfection are not the same thing. Removing visible dirt does not necessarily eliminate microorganisms, and disinfectants may not work properly on surfaces that have not been cleaned first.

2. Metal Equipment

Metal equipment is generally easier to clean than porous materials, but the appropriate cleaning method depends on its finish and construction.

  • Use compatible cleaning and disinfecting products.
  • Pay attention to joints, hinges, and attachment points.
  • Dry equipment to help prevent corrosion.
  • Inspect for rust, pitting, and damaged finishes.
  • Follow manufacturer instructions for lubrication and maintenance.

3. Wood Equipment

Wood can absorb moisture and may be damaged by excessive water or inappropriate cleaning chemicals.

  • Use cleaning products approved for the wood finish.
  • Avoid soaking wooden surfaces.
  • Dry surfaces appropriately after cleaning.
  • Inspect for cracks, splinters, and deterioration.
  • Maintain protective finishes according to manufacturer guidance.

Unsealed or damaged wood can be particularly difficult to clean effectively. Equipment with compromised surfaces should be assessed before being returned to service.

4. Leather and Faux Leather

Leather and synthetic leather are common materials used in BDSM furniture, restraints, benches, and padded equipment.

These materials require particular attention because some cleaning chemicals can cause cracking, discoloration, or deterioration.

  • Use manufacturer-approved cleaning products.
  • Do not assume that all disinfectants are safe for leather.
  • Pay attention to seams, stitching, and folds.
  • Allow equipment to dry completely.
  • Inspect for cracking, peeling, and exposed padding.
  • Replace damaged or heavily worn surfaces when necessary.

Porous or damaged upholstery may be difficult to disinfect reliably. Where appropriate cleaning cannot be assured, the equipment should not be shared until it has been professionally cleaned, repaired, or replaced.

5. Fabric, Straps, and Rope

Fabric equipment, straps, and ropes can retain sweat, moisture, oils, and other contaminants.

  • Follow the manufacturer's washing instructions.
  • Use appropriate detergents and cleaning methods.
  • Allow equipment to dry completely before storage.
  • Inspect stitching, fibers, and attachment hardware.
  • Remove equipment from service if it becomes damaged or contaminated beyond what can be safely cleaned.

Not every rope or strap can be disinfected using ordinary surface-cleaning products. Some materials require specialized cleaning or should be dedicated to a single person.

6. Cleaning Padded Furniture

Padded furniture can be more challenging to maintain because fluids may enter seams, stitching, and damaged upholstery.

  • Use compatible cleaning products.
  • Pay attention to seams and upholstery edges.
  • Inspect for tears and exposed foam.
  • Replace disposable protective coverings between users.
  • Allow surfaces to dry before placing them back into service.

Damaged padding or upholstery that cannot be properly cleaned should be repaired or replaced.

7. Routine Maintenance

Cleaning is only one part of maintaining dungeon equipment. Regular inspections and preventive maintenance help identify problems before they create safety concerns.

  • Inspect structural connections and fasteners.
  • Check hinges, locking mechanisms, and adjustment points.
  • Inspect restraints for wear and damage.
  • Check suspension hardware and attachment systems.
  • Maintain moving parts according to manufacturer instructions.
  • Keep maintenance and inspection records for critical equipment.
  • Remove questionable equipment from service until properly assessed.

Safe Positioning and Physical Considerations

Dungeon equipment can place the body in positions that are unfamiliar or physically demanding. Restraints and positioning furniture may restrict movement, create pressure points, or place stress on joints and muscles.

Equipment should accommodate the participant rather than forcing the participant to accommodate the equipment.

Important Physical Considerations

  • Circulation: Restraints should not create dangerous compression of blood vessels or compromise circulation.
  • Nerve compression: Pressure around wrists, elbows, shoulders, knees, and other areas can cause nerve injuries.
  • Joint positioning: Avoid forcing joints beyond their comfortable range of motion.
  • Breathing: Positioning should never compromise the ability to breathe freely.
  • Pressure points: Prolonged pressure against hard surfaces or restraint hardware can cause injuries.
  • Mobility limitations: Consider flexibility, previous injuries, chronic pain, and individual mobility requirements.
  • Fatigue: Remaining in one position for extended periods can create additional physical risks.

Participants should be monitored throughout a scene. Do not assume that someone will always recognize or communicate an injury immediately.

Equipment should be released or repositioned if someone experiences concerning pain, numbness, tingling, loss of sensation, difficulty breathing, or other signs of physical distress.

Accessibility and Accommodating Different Bodies

Not everyone has the same physical abilities, flexibility, mobility, or comfort with particular positions. Dungeon equipment should not be treated as having a single correct way to accommodate every participant.

People may have different needs related to disability, chronic pain, previous injuries, body size, age, mobility, or sensory differences.

Making Equipment More Accessible

  • Adjustable furniture: Equipment with adjustable heights and positioning can accommodate a wider range of bodies.
  • Support cushions and wedges: These may provide additional support and help reduce uncomfortable pressure points.
  • Alternative positions: A scene does not need to use the traditional position associated with a particular piece of equipment.
  • Accessible pathways: Keep routes around equipment clear for mobility devices and people who need additional space.
  • Communication: Ask participants what accommodations they need rather than making assumptions.
  • Appropriate equipment ratings: Ensure that equipment is suitable for the participant and intended application.

A good dungeon environment recognizes that participation should not be limited to people who fit a particular physical ideal. Flexibility and accommodation can make BDSM spaces more inclusive.

Consent and Negotiating Equipment Use

Using dungeon equipment introduces additional considerations that should be discussed before a scene begins.

Just because a piece of furniture is available does not mean someone has agreed to use it. Participants should understand what equipment will be involved and what physical positions or restrictions it may introduce.

Before Using Equipment, Discuss:

  • Which piece of equipment will be used.
  • What activities will take place.
  • Whether participants have previous experience with it.
  • Any relevant physical limitations or injuries.
  • How restraints or attachment systems will be used.
  • How the participant will communicate discomfort.
  • How the equipment can be released quickly.
  • What signals will indicate a pause or stop.
  • How long someone is expected to remain in a position.
  • What aftercare may be required.

A common safeword system is:

  • Green: Comfortable and willing to continue.
  • Yellow: Pause, check in, or make an adjustment.
  • Red: Stop immediately.

If a participant's position or restraints interfere with speech, establish an appropriate nonverbal communication method before beginning.

Consent is ongoing. Either participant can request an adjustment, pause, or complete stop at any point. Equipment availability and previous agreements do not override someone's right to withdraw consent.

Common Equipment Mistakes

Many equipment-related problems occur because people make assumptions about how something is designed or how much stress it can safely handle.

  • Assuming everything is load-bearing: A decorative hook or furniture attachment is not automatically suitable for suspension.
  • Using equipment for unintended purposes: Furniture designed for sitting or leaning should not automatically be used for suspension or pulling.
  • Ignoring equipment damage: Small cracks, loose hardware, and worn restraints can become serious safety problems.
  • Using incompatible hardware: Components designed for different applications may not work safely together.
  • Leaving someone unattended: Restrained participants may need assistance if discomfort, distress, or an emergency occurs.
  • Ignoring body positioning: A position that appears comfortable initially may become painful or unsafe over time.
  • Inadequate cleaning: Shared equipment should be cleaned using appropriate methods between participants.
  • Improvised repairs: Temporary fixes should never be used to return compromised structural or restraint equipment to service.
  • Assuming experience replaces inspection: Even experienced players should inspect equipment and follow established safety procedures.

Dungeon Equipment Etiquette

In addition to physical safety, using equipment in a shared dungeon requires respect for the venue, its equipment, and other attendees.

Shared Equipment Expectations

  • Ask permission before using equipment, even if it appears unoccupied.
  • Never interrupt someone else's scene to claim or use the equipment they are occupying.
  • Clean equipment after use according to venue procedures.
  • Do not modify, move, or disassemble equipment without permission.
  • Report damage, missing hardware, or safety concerns to venue staff.
  • Do not leave restraints, ropes, or personal belongings scattered around shared spaces.
  • Respect equipment reservations and scheduled use arrangements.
  • Return shared accessories and cleaning supplies to their designated locations.
  • Never assume that another person's personal equipment is available for general use.

Treat shared dungeon equipment as you would want someone else to treat your own. Responsible use and proper cleaning help ensure that equipment remains available and safe for the entire community.

Final Thoughts: Equipment Is Only as Safe as Its Use

Dungeon equipment can open up many different ways to explore BDSM, sensation, restraint, and power exchange. From a traditional St. Andrew's cross to a specialized suspension rig, each piece of equipment has its own purpose and considerations.

Understanding how equipment works is important, but recognizing its limitations is equally important. A piece of equipment that looks sturdy is not necessarily safe for every activity, every body, or every configuration.

Responsible equipment use involves proper inspection, appropriate training, ongoing communication, cleaning, maintenance, and respect for the people using it.

You do not need to be an expert on every piece of dungeon furniture. You do need to understand when something is outside your experience and when to ask for help.

Respect the equipment, understand its limitations, take care of the space, and always put the safety and autonomy of the people involved first.
